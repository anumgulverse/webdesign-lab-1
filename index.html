Emily Dickinson

Emily Dickinson, 1830–1886.

"Hope is the thing with feathers
That perches in the soul."

1. Growing Up in Amherst

Born in Amherst, Massachusetts, in 1830, Emily Dickinson spent most of her life in the town where she grew up. She studied at Amherst Academy and later attended Mount Holyoke Female Seminary. She enjoyed reading and spending time in nature.

2. The People Who Shaped Her Life

Emily grew up with her brother Austin and her younger sister Lavinia who later discovered many of Emily's unpublished poems after her death. Her sister's decision to preserve these writings helped introduce Emily's work to readers. Emily also had a close relationship with Susan Gilbert, who became Austin's wife. Although Emily lived a quiet and private life, her letters show that she had strong feelings and meaningful connections with the people around her.

3. Writing Style

Emily often used short lines, unusual punctuation, capital letters and rhymes that did not always sound exact. Her use of dashes gave her poems a distinctive rhythm and allowed ideas to remain open to interpretation. She could take something simple, like a bird singing or a walk through the countryside, and turn it into a reflection on hope, freedom or human life.

4. Why Death Appeared So Often in Her Poems?

Death was one of the subjects Emily Dickinson returned to throughout her writing. During her lifetime, she experienced loss and lived in a society where religion and ideas about the afterlife were important parts of everyday life. Instead of writing about death in only a frightening way, she explored it from different points of view.
